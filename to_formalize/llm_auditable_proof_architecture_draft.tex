\title[Structural Exhaustion for Human--AI PDE Proofs]
{Structural Exhaustion: Auditable Local-to-Global Estimates for Human--AI PDE Proofs}
\author{Guillem Duran Ballester}
\address{Fragile Technologies}
\email{guillem@fragile.tech}
\date{}
\subjclass[2020]{Primary 68T01; Secondary 35Q30, 35B65, 68V15}
\keywords{AI-assisted mathematics, structural exhaustion, local-to-global estimates, loss of compactness, partial differential equations, Stokes equations}

\begin{document}
\raggedbottom

\begin{abstract}
We introduce \emph{structural exhaustion}, a human--AI methodology for deriving
global analytic conclusions from exhaustive systems of local branches.  A
branch records its space--time window, quantitative hypotheses, gauge and
compactness choices, active defect, target estimate, monotone resources, and
supporting evidence.  A transition either proves a local estimate or converts
its first failure into one of finitely many structured defects.  Each defect is
then closed by a local estimate, a capacity comparison, a decrease of a
well-founded resource, or a rigidity contradiction.  Exhaustion of all defect
branches yields the global estimate; it is not assumed as an external input.

The formalism separates logical coverage, quantitative propagation, and
termination.  A finite closure theorem transports local bounds to the root.  A
first-failure compactness theorem shows that if every normalized counterexample
sequence enters a terminal contradiction branch, then a uniform estimate
holds.  A resource theorem makes exhaustion finite when every nonclosing route
decreases a discrete rank or consumes a fixed amount of a bounded quantity.
Branch-preserving repair remains available: if a step asserting \(P\) fails,
its descendants survive on the \(P\)-branch while \(\neg P\) becomes a new
structured route.

We give a finite-energy Stokes example in which standard local solenoidal
estimates are assembled with a pressure decomposition and an explicit audit of
the only entire divergence--curl kernel.  Finite energy and an expanding-ball
Caccioppoli estimate exclude that kernel, after which the equation closes the
harmonic pressure tail.  Three companion Navier--Stokes proof graphs then serve as a
case study in routing concentration, scale and translation escape, profile
splitting, weak-to-strong convergence defects, and pressure-gauge defects to
local remedies or resource-decreasing successors.  They illustrate the
methodology and do not assert a Navier--Stokes regularity theorem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Long proofs in nonlinear PDE are difficult to develop with language models for
a reason that is only partly linguistic.  Their conclusions depend on choices
that remain implicit in ordinary prose: the cylinder on which an estimate is
valid, the scale and frame, the pressure gauge, the quotient representative,
the topology used at a compactness limit, and the alternative left after an
estimate fails.  A locally plausible paragraph can silently change one of
these choices and thereby change the theorem.

Current AI systems for mathematical reasoning emphasize formal proof search,
autoformalization, retrieval, and repair of rejected proof scripts
\cite{WuEtAl2022,JiangEtAl2022,FirstEtAl2023,LuEtAl2024}.
Recent agentic systems recursively decompose informal arguments into formal
subgoals or interleave informal exploration with Lean verification
\cite{VaramballyEtAl2025,OspanovEtAl2025,AchimEtAl2025}.  Structural exhaustion
addresses an earlier analytic decomposition: before a PDE subgoal can be sent
to a verifier, its window, topology, gauge, failure mechanism, and quantitative
resource must be fixed.
Recent PDE-facing systems instead translate control requests or generate
numerical solvers \cite{SorocoEtAl2025,LiEtAl2025}.  These are important but
different problems.  The present paper addresses the intermediate activity in
which a mathematician and an AI system jointly construct a rigorous analytic
argument whose decisive estimates may remain in conventional mathematical
language.

Structural exhaustion represents the proof as an exhaustive directed graph of
local analytic states.  Nodes are not fragments of prose.  Each node exposes
the window, normalization, topology, quantitative budget, and active defect
required to interpret its claim.  Each edge either propagates an estimate or
extracts structured information from its first failure.  A terminal supplies a
local bound or contradiction.  When every failure route terminates and the
constants or charges are controlled along the graph, the terminal conclusions
assemble into a global estimate.  The representation is especially natural for
concentration compactness, profile decompositions, local pressure splittings,
scale selection, and rigidity arguments, where the analysis already advances
by alternatives
\cite{Lions1984a,Lions1984b,Gerard1998,BahouriGerard1999,Keraani2001}.

The constructive step is to use failure as data.  If compactness in a topology
\(X\) is needed downstream, its failure is not recorded as a generic gap.  It
must yield an exhaustive alternative such as concentration, escape of scale or
center, profile splitting, a nonzero defect measure, or loss of a pressure
representative.  Each alternative carries its inherited bounds and is routed
to a local estimate, a new normalized extraction, a finite-capacity account,
or rigidity.  Thus a missing monolithic estimate can be replaced by local
inequalities whose failure modes are structurally exhausted.

The same representation gives a stable repair rule.  Suppose a node was
intended to establish properties \(X\) and \(Y\), and every later step uses only
the declared interface \(X\wedge Y\).  Discovering that the attempted proof
does not establish \(Y\) leaves the conditional continuation intact.  The
preceding case is replaced by the exhaustive alternatives
\[
  \neg X,\qquad X\wedge\neg Y,\qquad X\wedge Y.
\]
The existing continuation is attached unchanged to the third alternative.
The first two alternatives are new analytic routes.  Their closure may use
precisely the same first-failure and resource mechanisms that produced the
original estimate.  We call this operation \emph{branch-preserving repair}.

The paper makes four contributions.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item It defines a PDE-specific structural-exhaustion state carrying local
  windows, quantitative interfaces, compactness data, and monotone resources.
  \item It proves semantic soundness, quantitative local-to-global closure, a
  first-failure compactness principle, resource-controlled termination, their
  synthesis as an estimate principle, and branch-preserving repair.
  \item It gives an operational division of labor: AI proposes and maintains
  local branches, while the mathematician controls theorem statements,
  admissible imports, and the acceptance of analytic evidence.
  \item It supplies a finite-energy Stokes instantiation in which standard local
  velocity estimates, representative control, and harmonic pressure-tail closure
  are composed in one auditable graph.  Three larger Navier--Stokes graphs
  show how the same method routes distinct losses of compactness through local
  remedies and resource-decreasing successors.
\end{enumerate}

The method is deliberately lighter than formalization.  Every local inequality,
compactness extraction, and rigidity step still requires mathematical proof.
Its new role is compositional: it supplies a disciplined way to construct a
global estimate from those local ingredients when no suitable global theorem is
available.  Graph consistency alone is not evidence, but exhaustive,
quantitative closure is a proof.

\section{Structural-exhaustion states}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Semantic setting}

Fix a universe \(\calU\) of admissible mathematical objects.  An element may
be a weak solution, a rescaled sequence, a limiting profile, or a local
space--time configuration.  A theorem has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
  A(s)\Longrightarrow T(s),\qquad s\in\calU,
\end{equation}
where \(A\) is the theorem hypothesis and \(T\) its conclusion.  Direct and
contradiction arguments are both included: in the latter case, the active goal
is the emptiness of \(A\wedge\neg T\).

\begin{definition}[PDE branch state]\label{def:branch-state}
A branch state is a record
\[
  B=(W,H,\Gamma,\tau,O,Q,M,E).
\]
Here \(W\) fixes the local window, scale, and coordinate frame; \(H\) is the
list of analytic hypotheses already available on that window; \(\Gamma\)
records gauge, quotient, normalization, and convergence choices; \(\tau\) is
the active case predicate; and \(O\) is the qualitative goal on this case.
The quantitative interface \(Q=(q_B,d_B)\) consists of finite nonnegative
functions on \(\sem B\), recording a target quantity and its admissible input
datum.  The resource record \(M\) contains the energy, mass,
rank, scale count, or other monotone quantity used to control repeated failure.
Finally, \(E\) contains the mathematical evidence accepted for the preceding
implications.  The semantic region is
\[
  \sem B=\{s\in\calU: W(s)\wedge H(s)\wedge\Gamma(s)\wedge\tau(s)\}.
\]
The fields are mathematical data.  They may be written in prose, equations,
or a table; no particular serialization is part of the method.
\end{definition}

The separation between \(H\), \(\Gamma\), \(\tau\), \(Q\), and \(M\) matters
in PDE.  A
bound can survive rescaling while a gauge choice does not; a sequence can
converge weakly in the energy space without giving the strong convergence
needed for a nonlinear term; and a pressure statement can be meaningful only
modulo functions of time.  A defect extraction may preserve the analytic
hypotheses but consume critical mass or lower a scale rank.  Recording these
choices prevents a downstream argument from consuming a stronger statement or
more quantitative resource than its parent produced.

\begin{definition}[Valid transition]\label{def:transition}
A transition from a branch \(B\) to children \(B_1,\ldots,B_m\), written
\[
  B\Longrightarrow B_1\vee\cdots\vee B_m,
\]
is valid if its evidence establishes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transition-cover}
  \sem{B_i}\subseteq\sem B\quad(1\le i\le m),
  \qquad
  \sem B\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^m\sem{B_i}.
\end{equation}
The first condition is refinement and the second is exhaustiveness.  Children
may overlap, although disjoint alternatives are preferable for diagnosis.  A
one-child transition is an implication; a multi-child transition is a case
split.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Quantitative transition]\label{def:quantitative-transition}
Let \(B\Longrightarrow B_1\vee\cdots\vee B_m\) be valid.  It is a
\emph{routing estimate} if its evidence supplies nonnegative constants
\(a_B,b_{Bi},\kappa_{Bi}\) such that, for \(s\in\sem B\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:routing-estimate}
 q_B(s)
 \le a_Bd_B(s)
 +\max_{i:\,s\in\sem{B_i}} b_{Bi}q_{B_i}(s),
 \qquad
 d_{B_i}(s)\le\kappa_{Bi}d_B(s).
\end{equation}
It is an \emph{aggregate estimate} if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aggregate-estimate}
 q_B(s)\le a_Bd_B(s)+\sum_{i=1}^m b_{Bi}q_{B_i}(s),
 \qquad
 \sum_{i=1}^m d_{B_i}(s)\le\kappa_Bd_B(s).
\end{equation}
In an aggregate estimate, \(q_{B_i}\) and \(d_{B_i}\) are extended by zero
outside \(\sem{B_i}\).
The second inequality in \eqref{eq:aggregate-estimate} includes the overlap or
multiplicity bound needed to sum local estimates.  A transition may carry both
interfaces when it first chooses a case and then decomposes its local pieces.
\end{definition}

Every transition also declares an \emph{output interface}: the facts that its
children may use.  A proof below the transition is interface-respecting when
it uses the transition only through those facts, not through an unstated detail
of the argument that produced them.  This familiar modularity condition is
what makes branch-preserving repair possible.

\begin{definition}[Structurally exhausted proof graph]\label{def:closed-graph}
A finite rooted directed acyclic graph is structurally exhausted for
\eqref{eq:target} if:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \(\{s:A(s)\}\subseteq\sem{B_0}\) for its root \(B_0\);
  \item every nonterminal node has a valid transition to all of its children;
  \item every terminal \(L\) has accepted evidence for
  \(\sem L\subseteq\{s:T(s)\}\).
\end{enumerate}
A terminal may close by a direct estimate, a capacity contradiction, a
rigidity theorem, or identification with an explicitly classified exceptional
case.  For a quantitative conclusion it also records a bound
\(q_L\le c_Ld_L\), or proves that \(\sem L\) contains no normalized failure.
\end{definition}

The graph is both a proof presentation and a composition rule for estimates.
Logical coverage, local analytic evidence, resource consumption, and constant
propagation remain separately reviewable.

\subsection{PDE transitions}

The states used in this paper admit five recurring transitions.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A \emph{localization} replaces a global object by nested cylinders or
  frequency windows and records every cutoff error.
  \item A \emph{decomposition} splits velocity, pressure, profiles, scales, or
  defects into named components and retains the remainder.
  \item An \emph{estimate} proves a local inequality and records its constant,
  data dependence, and overlap multiplicity.
  \item A \emph{compactness or rigidity step} changes the state only in the
  topology actually obtained and records the property transported to the
  limit.
  \item A \emph{decision} introduces exhaustive positive and negative cases
  for a property not yet known; its negative case identifies a defect or a
  resource-decreasing successor.
\end{enumerate}

For each node a reader should be able to answer eight questions: what case is
active, what inputs are available, which target quantity is controlled, which
resource is retained or consumed, what new fact is proved, which result proves
it, which alternatives leave the node, and where each alternative closes.  In
the Stokes example these questions are answered by
\Cref{tab:stokes-node-audit}; the larger Navier--Stokes instances use the same
format.

\subsection{Human--AI division of labor}

\begin{protocol}[Structural-exhaustion loop]\label{prot:loop}
Starting from \eqref{eq:target}, the collaborators repeat the following steps.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The mathematician fixes the theorem, admissible solution class, and
  which external results may be cited.
  \item The AI proposes the next local estimate or exhaustive split, together
  with its input/output interface, constant dependence, resource change, and a
  source-level argument.
  \item The mathematician accepts, rejects, or weakens that argument.  Simple
  graph checks may verify numbering, acyclicity, coverage declarations, and
  source links, but acceptance of an analytic estimate remains mathematical
  review.
  \item An accepted transition is added.  A rejected assertion \(P\) is
  replaced by the split \(P\vee\neg P\); the existing continuation is retained
  on \(P\), while \(\neg P\) is converted into a defect state whose local data
  and resource record determine the next route.
  \item The process stops only when every reachable branch has a terminal
  proof of the target.
\end{enumerate}
\end{protocol}

This allocation uses the model where it is effective: proposing decompositions,
searching for missing cases, restating local obligations, tracing citations,
and maintaining a large graph.  It leaves theorem ownership and analytic
judgment with the human author.  In particular, a fluent proof sketch is never
itself evidence that a branch is closed.

\section{Local-to-global correctness and repair}
\label{sec:correctness}

\begin{theorem}[Soundness]\label{thm:soundness}
If a finite graph is structurally exhausted for \eqref{eq:target}, then
\(A(s)\Rightarrow T(s)\) for every \(s\in\calU\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(s\) with \(A(s)\).  The root condition gives \(s\in\sem{B_0}\).
At every nonterminal node containing \(s\), exhaustiveness in
\eqref{eq:transition-cover} gives a child containing \(s\).  Finiteness and
acyclicity produce a terminal \(L\) with \(s\in\sem L\).  Terminal closure
then gives \(T(s)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Quantitative local-to-global closure]
\label{thm:quantitative-closure}
Let a finite structurally exhausted graph carry quantitative interfaces
\(Q_B=(q_B,d_B)\).  Suppose every nonterminal transition satisfies either
\eqref{eq:routing-estimate} or \eqref{eq:aggregate-estimate}, and every terminal
\(L\) satisfies
\[
  q_L(s)\le c_Ld_L(s),\qquad s\in\sem L.
\]
Then the root satisfies a uniform estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root-global-estimate}
  q_{B_0}(s)\le C_{\mathrm{root}}d_{B_0}(s),\qquad s\in\sem{B_0},
\end{equation}
where \(C_{\mathrm{root}}\) is determined by the terminal constants and the recorded
transition constants.  In particular, the global constant is an output of the
closed local graph.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Order the vertices backwards from the terminals.  Put \(C_L=c_L\) at a
terminal.  At a routing transition define
\[
 C_B=a_B+\max_i b_{Bi}\kappa_{Bi}C_{B_i},
\]
and at an aggregate transition define
\[
 C_B=a_B+\kappa_B\max_i b_{Bi}C_{B_i}.
\]
The routing formula follows by choosing a child containing \(s\), using its
bound, and transporting its datum through \eqref{eq:routing-estimate}.  For an
aggregate transition,
\[
 \sum_i b_{Bi}q_{B_i}
 \le \max_i(b_{Bi}C_{B_i})\sum_i d_{B_i}
 \le \kappa_B\max_i(b_{Bi}C_{B_i})d_B.
\]
Backward induction is valid because the graph is finite and acyclic, and gives
\eqref{eq:root-global-estimate}.
\end{proof}

The preceding theorem assembles inequalities already visible on the graph.  A
compactness argument often creates those inequalities indirectly by excluding
all normalized failures.

\begin{theorem}[First-failure compactness closure]
\label{thm:first-failure-compactness}
Let \(\calU\) be a cone and let \(q,d:\calU\to[0,\infty)\) be positively
homogeneous of the same degree \(\alpha>0\).  Assume that every sequence satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalized-failure}
 q(s_n)=1,
 \qquad d(s_n)\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}
admits a subsequence that enters a finite exhaustive extraction graph.  Each
edge of this graph must preserve the normalization and the topology, gauge, and
resource data required by its successor.  If every terminal contradicts
\eqref{eq:normalized-failure}, then there is a finite constant \(C\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compactness-generated-estimate}
 q(s)\le C d(s),\qquad s\in\calU.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If \eqref{eq:compactness-generated-estimate} failed, there would be \(u_n\) with
\(q(u_n)>n d(u_n)\).  Setting
\(s_n=q(u_n)^{-1/\alpha}u_n\) gives \(q(s_n)=1\) and
\(d(s_n)<1/n\).  Exhaustiveness routes a subsequence
to a terminal, while terminal closure says that no such normalized sequence can
reach that terminal.  This contradiction proves the estimate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Resource-controlled exhaustion]
\label{thm:resource-termination}
Consider a finitely branching structural-exhaustion tree.  Suppose every live
state carries a rank \(r\in\{0,\ldots,R\}\) and a budget
\(0\le\mu\le M\).  Along every nonclosing edge either \(r\) decreases, or \(r\)
is unchanged and \(\mu\) decreases by at least \(\delta>0\).  When \(r\)
decreases, \(\mu\) may be reset to at most \(M\).  Then every path has length at
most
\[
 (R+1)\bigl(1+\lfloor M/\delta\rfloor\bigr),
\]
and the tree is finite.  Consequently, if every terminal supplies a local
estimate or contradiction, structural exhaustion produces an unconditional
global conclusion.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At each fixed rank, at most \(\lfloor M/\delta\rfloor\) budget-consuming edges
are possible.  The rank can decrease at most \(R\) times.  The displayed bound
follows.  A finitely branching tree with uniformly bounded path length has
finitely many vertices.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Structural-exhaustion estimate principle]
\label{thm:structural-exhaustion-estimate}
Let \(\calU\) be a cone and let
\(q,d:\calU\to[0,\infty)\) be positively homogeneous of the same positive
degree.  Suppose that every normalized failure sequence
\[
 q(s_n)=1,\qquad d(s_n)\to0,
\]
enters a finitely branching extraction tree whose transitions preserve the
normalization and every topology, gauge, and quantitative interface consumed
downstream.  Assume further that:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item every nonclosing transition either lowers a rank
  \(r\in\{0,\ldots,R\}\) or, at fixed rank, consumes at least
  \(\delta>0\) from a budget bounded by \(M\); and
  \item every terminal contradicts the normalized failure conditions.
\end{enumerate}
Then the extraction tree is finite and there is a constant \(C<\infty\) such
that
\[
 q(s)\le C d(s),\qquad s\in\calU.
\]
If this estimate is the terminal interface of a finite quantitative proof
graph, \Cref{thm:quantitative-closure} propagates it, together with all recorded
overlap and transport constants, to the root.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The resource hypotheses and finite branching imply finiteness of the extraction
tree by \Cref{thm:resource-termination}.  It is therefore a finite exhaustive
extraction graph of the kind required in
\Cref{thm:first-failure-compactness}.  Terminal exclusion gives the asserted
estimate.  The last statement is exactly
\Cref{thm:quantitative-closure}.
\end{proof}

The same routing argument gives a sharp residual cover for an incomplete proof.

\begin{theorem}[Partial closure]\label{thm:partial}
Suppose every transition in a finite rooted graph is valid, but a set
\(\calL_{\mathrm{open}}\) of reachable leaves has not yet been closed.  If all
other leaves prove \(T\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial}
  \{s:A(s)\wedge\neg T(s)\}
  \subseteq
  \bigcup_{L\in\calL_{\mathrm{open}}}\sem L.
\end{equation}
Thus the open leaves, rather than an informal list of gaps, cover every
residual class in which a counterexample may remain.  Equality holds when the
root is exactly \(A\) and the branch alternatives form a disjoint partition.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Route a putative \(s\) with \(A(s)\wedge\neg T(s)\) from the root as in the
proof of \Cref{thm:soundness}.  It cannot reach a closed leaf, so it reaches an
open one.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Branch-preserving repair}

Dependency-graph language often suggests that a failed lemma invalidates its
forward dependency closure.  That is not the repair rule here.  The downstream
proofs are conditional arguments, and a failed attempt to establish their
input does not make those conditional arguments false.

\begin{theorem}[Branch-preserving repair]\label{thm:repair}
Let \(B\) be the last verified branch before a transition intended to establish
a predicate \(P\).  Assume every descendant of that transition is
interface-respecting: it uses the transition only through \(P\) and the facts
already present in \(B\).  If the evidence for \(P\) fails, replace the
transition by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:binary-repair}
  B\Longrightarrow B^+\vee B^- ,
  \qquad
  \sem{B^+}=\sem B\cap\{P\},
  \quad
  \sem{B^-}=\sem B\cap\{\neg P\}.
\end{equation}
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the replacement transition is valid independently of the failed
  evidence;
  \item the entire former descendant graph remains valid, without alteration,
  below \(B^+\);
  \item the only new proof obligation is \(B^-\); and
  \item if the former descendant graph closed \(B^+\), the repaired graph
  satisfies \eqref{eq:partial} with \(B^-\) as its additional residual class.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The two sets in \eqref{eq:binary-repair} refine \(\sem B\) and cover it by
excluded middle.  On \(B^+\), the predicate \(P\) is part of the case
definition.  Every former descendant therefore receives exactly the interface
it previously consumed, so its proof is unchanged.  No claim has been made on
\(B^-\); it is the new open leaf.  The final assertion is
\Cref{thm:partial}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Ordered first-failure repair]\label{cor:first-failure}
If the failed node asserted
\(P=X_1\wedge\cdots\wedge X_m\), it may be replaced by the disjoint exhaustive
split
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-failure}
\begin{aligned}
  &\neg X_1,\\
  &X_1\wedge\neg X_2,\\
  &\hspace{1.2em}\vdots\\
  &X_1\wedge\cdots\wedge X_{m-1}\wedge\neg X_m,\\
  &X_1\wedge\cdots\wedge X_m.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The former descendants remain on the final branch.  Each new branch records
the first absent property and can be analyzed independently.
\end{corollary}

For the motivating two-property example, discovering that \(Y\) is not
established creates the case \(X\wedge\neg Y\); it does not alter any theorem
proved under \(X\wedge Y\).  The additional case \(\neg X\) is retained when
\(X\) was also part of the unproved conjunctive node.  If \(X\) was already
verified earlier, that branch is empty and may be closed immediately.

\begin{theorem}[Monotone closure]\label{thm:monotone}
Assume repairs are made by \Cref{thm:repair} and accepted evidence is changed
only when its own statement or proof is found defective.  Closing or refining
one open branch does not reopen any other closed branch.  If every newly
created complementary branch is eventually closed, the resulting graph proves
the original theorem.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A repair partitions one semantic region and leaves all other regions and their
evidence unchanged.  Refinement preserves this separation.  When all leaves
are closed, \Cref{thm:soundness} applies.
\end{proof}

The role of dependency information is therefore diagnostic rather than
destructive.  It identifies the interface at which to insert the dichotomy and
the continuation to attach to its positive arm.  It does not mark a forward
set of mathematical statements as invalid.

Three constraints are particularly important.

\paragraph{Locality.}
An interior estimate consumes data on an explicitly larger window and produces
a conclusion on an explicitly smaller one.  Cutoff errors and pressure
remainders are children of the same node unless a cited estimate controls them.

\paragraph{Representative stability.}
Statements involving pressure, harmonic gradients, potentials, or quotient
spaces specify the representative that survives the transition.  Equality of
vorticity does not, by itself, identify velocity; weak convergence of pressure
does not choose its time-dependent additive constant.

\paragraph{Topological sufficiency.}
Each limit passage names the convergence needed by the next nonlinear
operation.  A missing strong convergence statement is handled as a negative
branch, not silently upgraded from a weak one.

These constraints turn common review questions into branch questions.  If a
profile decomposition omits a scale relation, split on that relation.  If a
pressure estimate controls only the nonharmonic component, retain the harmonic
component as a branch.  If a rigidity lemma requires \(X\wedge Y\), use the
ordered alternatives in \eqref{eq:first-failure}.  In each case the successful
continuation remains available while the complementary analytic class becomes
the next research target.

This is the connection between structural exhaustion and loss of compactness.
A compactness step declares the limit object, its normalization, the topology
of convergence, the properties transported to the limit, and the resource
retained after extraction.  If one part of this interface fails, the first
failure is converted into positive structure: concentration, escape of scale or
center, profile splitting, a nonzero defect measure, or loss of a pressure
representative.  The successful compactness continuation remains available,
while each defect is routed to a new local estimate, normalization, capacity
comparison, or rigidity test.

The method therefore replaces a missing global compactness estimate by an
exhaustive local construction.  A concentration branch consumes a fixed amount
of critical mass; an escaping branch changes frame or becomes locally
invisible; a splitting branch produces finitely many active profiles; a
weak-to-strong defect produces another core; and a gauge defect is separated
from the velocity limit and closed elliptically.  If these routes exhaust the
normalized failures, \Cref{thm:first-failure-compactness} yields the estimate.
If every continuing route also decreases a rank or consumes a bounded resource,
\Cref{thm:resource-termination} makes the exhaustion finite.  The methodology
is thus the local-to-global tool: it constructs the global conclusion from
local analytic mechanisms rather than presupposing a theorem that already
contains it.

\section{Complete Stokes instantiation}
\label{sec:stokes}

The example now isolates the nonlocal pressure issue that occurs in the
Navier--Stokes developments while removing the nonlinear term.  On
\(\R^3\times(0,T)\) consider
\begin{equation}\label{stokes:eq:local}
  \partial_tu-\Delta u+\nabla p=-\divergence F,
  \qquad \divergence u=0,
\end{equation}
where \((\divergence F)_i=\partial_jF_{ij}\).  The pressure is defined modulo
a distribution depending only on time.

\begin{definition}[Finite-energy Stokes state]\label{stokes:def:energy-state}
A finite-energy weak solution of \eqref{stokes:eq:local} satisfies
\[
 u\in L^\infty_{\loc}(0,T;L^2(\R^3))
 \cap L^2_{\loc}(0,T;\dot H^1(\R^3)),
 \qquad p\in\mathcal D'(\R^3\times(0,T)),
\]
and the equation holds in distributions.  We assume
\(F\in C^\infty(\R^3\times(0,T))\), with its derivatives locally square
integrable in time and space.  This is the linear analogue of the
finite-energy class used in the Navier--Stokes applications.
\end{definition}

Fix \(B_r\Subset B_\rho\Subset B_R\), choose
\(\chi\in C_c^\infty(B_R)\) with \(\chi=1\) on \(B_\rho\), and let
\(N(x)=(4\pi|x|)^{-1}\), so \(-\Delta N=\delta_0\).  Define the compact-source
pressure and the pressure tail by
\begin{equation}\label{stokes:eq:pressure-split}
 p_{\mathrm{near}}=\partial_i\partial_jN*(\chi F_{ij}),
 \qquad p_{\mathrm{tail}}=p-p_{\mathrm{near}}-c(t),
\end{equation}
where the time distribution \(c\) fixes the gauge, for example by requiring
\(\langle p_{\mathrm{tail}}(\cdot,t),\varphi\rangle_x=0\) for a fixed
\(\varphi\in C_c^\infty(B_\rho)\) with \(\int\varphi=1\).  Then
\(\Delta p_{\mathrm{tail}}=0\) on \(B_\rho\).  The tail contains all influence
not represented by the compactly supported local source; its regularity will
be proved, not assumed.

\begin{remark}[Why finite energy is essential]\label{stokes:rem:counterexample}
For a nonsmooth scalar function \(a(t)\),
\[
 u(x,t)=a(t)e_1,\qquad p(x,t)=-a'(t)x_1,\qquad F=0
\]
is a local distributional Stokes solution with zero vorticity.  It is excluded
from \Cref{stokes:def:energy-state} unless \(a=0\), because a nonzero spatially
constant velocity is not in \(L^2(\R^3)\).  Thus a local curl argument alone
cannot prove the theorem below; the finite-energy branch must be used.
\end{remark}

For nested cylinders \(Q_r\Subset Q_R\) and an integer \(m\ge0\), set
\begin{align}
 q_m(Q_r;u)
 &:=\sum_{2j+|\alpha|\le m}
 \|\partial_t^j\partial_x^\alpha u\|_{L^2(Q_r)},
 \label{stokes:eq:q-interface}\\
 d_m(Q_R;u,F)
 &:=\|u\|_{L^2(J_R;L^2(\R^3))}
   +\mathcal N_{m,R,J_R}(F).
 \label{stokes:eq:d-interface}
\end{align}
Here \(\mathcal N_{m,R,J_R}(F)\) denotes a fixed finite sum of local
\(L^2\)-norms of the derivatives of \(F\) required up to parabolic order \(m\).
These are the quantitative interface of the Stokes graph.  Its resource record
is the finite derivative rank together with the remaining cylinder buffer.
\Cref{stokes:lem:local-bootstrap} supplies the local transition
\(q_m(Q_r;u)\le C_m d_m(Q_R;u,F)\), including its buffer and data dependence.
The whole-space finite-energy hypothesis has a separate role: it excludes every
entire divergence--curl ambiguity by
\Cref{stokes:lem:finite-energy-kernel}.  The example therefore does not infer a
global bound for normalized difference quotients from the energy of \(u\); it
composes a proved local estimate with a proved global representative exclusion.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-energy Stokes regularity by local pressure-tail closure]
\label{stokes:thm:main}
Let \((u,p,F)\) be a finite-energy Stokes state on
\(\R^3\times(0,T)\).  Then
\[
 u\in C^\infty(\R^3\times(0,T)),
 \qquad \nabla p\in C^\infty(\R^3\times(0,T)).
\]
For every compact cylinder \(Q'\Subset\R^3\times(0,T)\) and every derivative
order, the corresponding norms of \(u\) and \(\nabla p\) are bounded by the
finite-energy norm of \(u\) on a larger time interval and finitely many local
norms of derivatives of \(F\) on a buffered spatial cylinder.  For every split
\eqref{stokes:eq:pressure-split},
\(\nabla p_{\mathrm{tail}}\in C^\infty(B_r\times I_r)\).
\end{theorem}

The proof uses no whole-space pressure formula and no global heat or Oseen
kernel.  Compactly supported divergence corrections remove pressure from the
velocity estimates and record their constants on nested cylinders.  The
finite-energy kernel lemma separately excludes an unrecorded entire harmonic
representative.  With these two obligations closed, the equation controls the
harmonic pressure tail on the target cylinder.

\subsection{How to read the proof architecture}

Node [2] records the local near/tail pressure split without assigning any time
regularity to the harmonic tail.  Curl and local heat estimates give the
smooth vorticity in [3].  Solenoidal cutoffs and annular Bogovski\u\i{}
corrections give the quantitative local bootstrap in [4], hence smoothness of
the raw velocity in [5].  Independently, [6] excludes the entire finite-energy
divergence--curl kernel, so [7] certifies that no global harmonic representative
was lost.  Node [8] then uses the original equation to prove smoothness of the
pressure tail, and [9] gives full regularity of the unmodified velocity and
pressure gradient.

mechanisms and exhaustive loss-of-compactness routes.

The companion developments are not independently validated here as a proof of
global regularity, and this paper draws no Navier--Stokes regularity conclusion
from them.  Their role is to expose, at realistic scale, the state interfaces,
resources, and closure routes that an LLM must maintain.  Branch-preserving
repair retains a reviewed continuation on the positive arm of a failed
assertion, while structural exhaustion works on the defect arm using the same
local-to-global calculus.

The source repository also contains the broader structural-exhaustion development
from which the present PDE specialization is derived
\cite{DuranBallester2026}.  The present paper isolates the human--AI protocol,
the quantitative closure principles, and the PDE state variables needed to
apply it.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the method guarantees}

Structural exhaustion has three levels of guarantee.  Semantic soundness says
that an exhaustive terminal graph proves the target on every admissible case.
Quantitative closure propagates local constants and overlap bounds to a root
estimate by \Cref{thm:quantitative-closure}.  First-failure compactness turns
exclusion of every normalized defect into the existence of a uniform constant
by \Cref{thm:first-failure-compactness}.  When continuing defects consume a
bounded resource or decrease a rank, \Cref{thm:resource-termination} also
proves that the construction ends.  \Cref{thm:structural-exhaustion-estimate}
combines these operations into the local-to-global estimate principle.  These guarantees are mathematical
consequences of the certified local transitions; the diagram records their
composition.

If some leaves remain open, \Cref{thm:partial} identifies the residual classes
in which a counterexample may remain.  If an asserted property fails,
\Cref{thm:repair} preserves the conditional mathematics below that property and
routes its complement through the structural-exhaustion loop.  These guarantees
do not depend on a particular model or proof assistant.

The representation also supports useful automated checks: node identifiers can
be compared with ledger rows; terminal shapes can be checked for outgoing
edges; decision nodes can be required to list all alternatives; source links
can be resolved; and state fields can be compared across transitions.  Such
checks detect structural omissions.  They do not decide whether a cited PDE
estimate is applicable.

For the Stokes graph included here, the repository audit verifies the contiguous
range [1]--[9], nine solid nodes, one solid terminal, no closure proxies, the
permitted number of outgoing edges for every node shape, and an audit-table row
for every node.  This is structural evidence only; the source ledger and the
proofs of its cited lemmas provide the mathematical evidence.

\subsection{Limitations}

Structural exhaustion requires an exhaustive defect alphabet and a progress
mechanism.  A problem lies outside a proposed instantiation when a normalized
failure produces a defect not represented by the state language, when a local
transition has no proved estimate, or when continuing branches have no
well-founded rank or bounded resource.  These are precise mathematical design
obligations.  The method can replace a missing global theorem by composing
local tools, but it cannot replace the proofs of the local dichotomies,
compactness statements, capacity bounds, and rigidity results that make the
composition valid.

A poor state choice can also create unnecessary branches or lose uniform
constants.  Interface-respecting descendants are essential: if a later proof
uses an unstated feature of an extraction, it must be rewritten against an
explicit topology, gauge, and quantitative interface.  Aggregate transitions
must record overlap and multiplicity; otherwise summing valid local estimates
need not give a valid global estimate.

The Stokes example is linear and its local cutoff estimates and global kernel
exclusion are explicit.
The Navier--Stokes instances are substantially larger, but they do not constitute a
controlled user study.  Measuring whether the protocol improves correctness,
review time, or AI contribution quality is future work.  A useful evaluation
would compare ordinary prose development with structural-exhaustion development on
the same PDE problems and record omitted cases, repair cost, and expert review
time.

\section{Conclusion}

Structural exhaustion gives human and AI collaborators a common unit of work:
a local analytic state with a quantitative interface, a monotone resource, and
an exhaustive continuation.  Its central operation is constructive.  Failure
of a proposed global estimate is normalized, localized, and partitioned into
defect mechanisms.  Each mechanism supplies additional structure that can be
estimated, charged, rescaled, compactified, or excluded by rigidity.  Closing
all routes produces the global estimate, while resource consumption makes the
closure finite.  Branch-preserving repair is a complementary benefit: a broken
step preserves its conditional continuation and sends only its defect arm back
through the same calculus.

The Stokes example shows why this matters even in a linear PDE: a local
pressure split leaves a harmonic tail, while vorticity smoothing leaves a
possible entire divergence--curl ambiguity.  The local solenoidal estimate
controls the raw velocity, and finite energy excludes the global ambiguity by
an expanding-ball Caccioppoli argument.  The equation then closes the pressure
tail.  The Navier--Stokes case study applies the same design to
concentration, translation and scale escape, profile splitting,
weak-to-strong defects, and pressure gauges.  It shows how local analytic
mechanisms can be assembled into global control without asserting that the
companion branches constitute a validated regularity proof.

This is the role of structural exhaustion in AI-assisted PDE mathematics: the
model maintains the large local state space and proposes typed failure routes;
the proof derives its force from exhaustive local estimates, quantitative
resource accounting, and terminal analytic contradictions.  The methodology
thereby occupies the space between an unsupported global guess and a fully
formal proof script.
