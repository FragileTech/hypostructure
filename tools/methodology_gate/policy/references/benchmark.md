# Controlled mathematical reasoning benchmark

This workflow benchmarks structural mathematical reasoning: identifying relevant structure, applying established textbook mathematics, and implementing the resulting deductions in an unfamiliar controlled setting. Execute the assigned local task from its supplied hypotheses and accepted results. Assess the actual inference required; the surrounding research topic does not determine the difficulty of that inference.

Identify the retained objects, match the hypotheses of the relevant textbook result, execute the deduction, and check the requested output. Give routine steps concise, sufficient justification. Reuse accepted prerequisites at their stated types and domains. A review objection identifies a concrete missing hypothesis, invalid inference, domain mismatch, or unmet task contract and its local repair. Preserve the assigned objectives, stage boundaries, mathematical statements, and required checks.

Use the exact current assignment and its permitted mathematical sources.
Accepted evidence supplies propositions at their actual statements. Evaluate
the local inference from those propositions; the reputation of the parent
problem supplies no extra proof obligation. An unfamiliar arrangement of
standard objects is the intended setting for the benchmark.

A task completes when its specified result has accepted evidence. A productive
move meets the existing payoff requirements on every outcome. A branch closes
when accepted results compose to its exact endpoint. Keep these completion
units and the existing execution/review sequence unchanged.

For a routine consequence, give the deduction directly. For a textbook theorem,
state its relevant hypotheses and match them to the bound objects. For a missing
premise, state the exact missing proposition and return it through the existing
repair or decomposition mechanism. Review only the assigned deliverable.

Runs and reviews are bound to the current benchmark policy. Check the policy
fingerprint before using their evidence or dispatching an assignment. Read
mathematical inputs at their exact source statements and verified hashes.
