# [144a] choose the next tension on the marked return

The branch remains the full literal [144a] selected-graph conjunction. The current extremal two-tail argument has exposed one actual `e={a,x}∉F` and a simple return `P_e` in `G−e` for each matching/star producer. That fact interacts with two retained restrictions on the *same* source witness: the chosen pair of routes maximizes its common-prefix length among valid configurations for its fixed source pairs and demands, and each route must stay in its own pair-specific support and land in its own local buffer. Earlier nodes used those restrictions to exclude an all-in-`F` shortcut. They did not test the newly forced `P_e` against them.

**Candidate tension.** Mark every first and later encounter of `P_e` with the two selected routes. On each intervening segment, keep membership in both original supports and the positions of the fixed root and terminal buffers. If an encounter and segment can participate in a *valid* same-pair reroute with a longer common prefix, maximality rules it out. Otherwise the first failed prerequisite is a concrete geometric or support constraint on the marked return: no useful re-entry, a vertex/edge outside the relevant support, a wrong endpoint or buffer, or a repetition preventing a simple path. These outcomes are only a prospective partition; none is yet proved to pay a distinct unit or close the residual. They preserve the original source pair and entire ledger.

**Serious rival.** Charge `e` and its return cycle to the exact attachment/rank account. This already proves `β(G)≥β(F)+1` mathematically and, with `c(F)∈{1,2}`, the net attachment inequality. It uses physical information absent from the old all-in-`F` pattern. But its entire certified payment is at least one unit for one selected envelope. The source pattern may have many pairs sharing the same `e` or exterior component, and no bounded-multiplicity map is retained. Reapplying the same rank inequality per source pair would reuse one unit. The two-component case additionally spends one raw attachment unit on a merger before the return cycle's rank appears. Thus the rival currently cannot consume the source overload or yield a cap.

The marked-route interaction is the better **structural tension to investigate next** because it uses a relation that the earlier generic cycle trace and rank identity did not measure: the *specific forced return* against the *specific extremal pair's* route validity. This is a choice of question, not of a proof technique or productive move. The rank account remains available as a separate consumer if a later argument proves distinctness or bounded multiplicity.

Four exception families are mandatory in the next move catalogue. (1) `P_e` may have no later encounter with the old route union, including when `x` is outside `S`. (2) It may meet a route but leave that route's exact pair-specific support or miss its fixed terminal buffer; support for `p` cannot be borrowed from `q`, even for a shared star demand. (3) Several source pairs may reuse one physical `e`, return path, or exterior component, so an index-by-index charge needs a fresh multiplicity theorem. (4) `F` can have two components; an edge merger alone is not a cycle-rank unit. Multiple encounters, overlapping arms/core, and equal landings stay explicit in the Phase 2 membership words.

The first missing inference is a local conditional implication: specify a return-path segment and its entry/exit positions on the original routes that would preserve *all* `RoutingConfiguration` fields and strictly increase the fixed pair's common prefix. The no-reentry and failed-validity arms then need their own proved restriction or cost. Until those implications are catalogued and checked on every arm, no shortcut move is admitted and the [144a] handoff remains open.
